1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & PROBLEM STATEMENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.1 Background & Context
Modern operational weather forecasting relies on massive Numerical Weather 
Prediction (NWP) systems, such as the 12 km NCMRWF Global Ensemble Prediction 
System (NEPS-G) and the deterministic NCUM model. In medium-range forecasting 
(3 to 10 days), non-linear atmospheric chaos produces wide ensemble spread, 
rendering single deterministic forecasts unreliable. Meteorologists must 
manually scan multi-terabyte, multivariable 4D datasets to identify evolving 
catastrophic weather anomalies (e.g., tropical cyclones, localized cloudbursts, 
severe heat domes, and cold waves).

1.2 The Core Problem: Spectral Smoothing
Existing deep learning approaches (such as conventional Convolutional Neural 
Networks and U-Nets) fail when applied directly to extreme weather downscaling. 
Because they optimize standard mean-error loss functions (Mean Squared Error or 
Mean Absolute Error), they suffer from severe "spectral smoothing." In effect, 
they average out spatial extremes, flattening peak wind gusts, torrential 
precipitation rates, and localized thermal anomalies. There remains a critical 
operational gap between coarse 12 km global ensemble grids and localized, 
high-fidelity threat tracking.

1.3 Proposed Solution Overview (Project VATAWARAN)
Project VATAWARAN provides an end-to-end automated climate intelligence engine 
utilizing a two-stage hybrid AI architecture:
  - Stage 1 (Spherical GNN Tracking): Ingests global ensemble fields directly 
    onto an icosahedral spherical mesh, calculating Extreme Forecast Index (EFI) 
    anomalies against a 30-year climatological baseline to isolate and track 
    moving 4D bounding boxes over a 3- to 10-day window.
  - Stage 2 (Amplitude-Preserving Diffusion Downscaling): Crops the isolated 
    anomaly zone and executes a physics-constrained conditional diffusion 
    model. This generates a hyper-local 5 km subgrid impact zone that preserves 
    true extreme amplitudes without regression blurring.
  - Delivery Layer: Translates 5 km centroid arrays into automated GIS layers 
    and machine-readable GeoJSON alerts for emergency services and agriculture.


2. TARGET AUDIENCE, USER PERSONAS & OPERATIONAL PAIN POINTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.1 Persona 1: Incident Commander / First Responder
    - Target Entity: National Disaster Response Force (NDRF), State Disaster 
      Management Authorities (SDMA).
    - Responsibilities: Pre-positioning rescue personnel, evacuating vulnerable 
      populations, dispatching heavy flood mitigation machinery.
    - Current Pain Point: "Alert Fatigue." Existing alerts are issued across 
      entire districts or states. Broad, non-specific warnings cause public 
      complacency and misallocation of emergency assets.
    - VATAWARAN Solution: Pinpoints a maximum-severity coordinate with a 5 km 
      high-risk radius, giving 24 to 72 hours of targeted tactical lead time.

2.2 Persona 2: District Agricultural Officer & Farming Cooperatives
    - Target Entity: Ministry of Agriculture & Farmers Welfare, Krishi Vigyan 
      Kendras (KVKs), Farmer Producer Organizations (FPOs).
    - Responsibilities: Issuing agro-meteorological advisories, managing 
      irrigation gates, organizing protective crop harvesting.
    - Current Pain Point: Sudden microclimate anomalies (frost, localized hail, 
      unseasonal downpours, flash heatwaves) destroy standing crops because 
      district-level forecasts fail to capture local geographic exposure.
    - VATAWARAN Solution: 3- to 10-day structural foresight at 5 km resolution, 
      enabling pre-harvest adjustments and crop protective coverings.

2.3 Persona 3: Operational Duty Forecaster
    - Target Entity: National Weather Prediction Centers (NCMRWF, IMD).
    - Responsibilities: Monitoring 50+ ensemble members across multiple runs 
      daily, issuing severe weather bulletins.
    - Current Pain Point: Manual slice-and-dice data triage across gigabytes of 
      GRIB2 files creates cognitive overload and delays warning dissemination.
    - VATAWARAN Solution: Automated anomaly trajectory extraction with 
      pre-computed dynamic bounding boxes and physical consistency metrics.

4. USER JOURNEYS & SCENARIOS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.1 Scenario A: Tropical Cyclone Landfall Prediction
  - Step 1: Upstream NEPS-G 00Z run arrives via automated pipeline.
  - Step 2: VATAWARAN's Stage 1 GNN scans the Bay of Bengal and flags an anomaly 
    with an EFI score of 0.94 at lead time T+96h.
  - Step 3: A dynamic 4D bounding box is computed, defining the cyclone center 
    and estimated track cone over the next 4 days.
  - Step 4: The forecaster reviews the track and initiates Stage 2 diffusion.
  - Step 5: The engine produces a 5 km localized wind-field and rain footprint 
    revealing a peak precipitation cell exceeding 280 mm/24h.
  - Step 6: Automated GeoJSON alert dispatched to state disaster headquarters.

4.2 Scenario B: Agricultural Flash Heatwave Alert
  - Step 1: Stage 1 GNN identifies a persistent 850 hPa geopotential ridge 
    over Northwest India at lead time T+120h.
  - Step 2: The system tracks the centroid of the thermal anomaly and crops a 
    500 km x 500 km subgrid over an agrarian belt.
  - Step 3: Stage 2 diffusion downscales the 2-meter temperature ($T_{2m}$), 
    incorporating local elevation profiles.
  - Step 4: Agricultural dashboards receive localized risk indices indicating 
    temperatures exceeding 44 deg C for 72 consecutive hours.
  - Step 5: FPO managers trigger automated SMS advisories recommending 
    emergency night-time irrigation to prevent crop desiccation.


5. FUNCTIONAL REQUIREMENTS (FR)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5.1 Module 1: Ingestion & Trajectory Tracking (Stage 1)
  - FR-1.1: The system shall ingest GRIB2 and NetCDF4 datasets containing 12 km 
    NEPS-G ensemble outputs across multi-pressure levels (1000 to 200 hPa).
  - FR-1.2: The system shall calculate the Extreme Forecast Index (EFI) by 
    referencing a pre-computed 30-year climatological baseline (ERA5 / IMDAA).
  - FR-1.3: The system shall project global lat-long grids onto a distortion-free 
    icosahedral spherical mesh representation.
  - FR-1.4: The system shall identify spatial clusters with EFI >= 0.80 and 
    generate a 4D spatio-temporal bounding box covering a 3- to 10-day window.

5.2 Module 2: Physics-Guided Downscaling (Stage 2)
  - FR-2.1: The system shall dynamically extract the multivariable atmospheric 
    tensor within the bounding box computed by Stage 1.
  - FR-2.2: The system shall downscale the cropped 12 km input array to a 5 km 
    spatial grid using a conditional denoising diffusion probabilistic model.
  - FR-2.3: The system shall condition generation on auxiliary topographic 
    data (SRTM DEM) and land-sea masks.
  - FR-2.4: The model loss function shall enforce physical conservation laws, 
    penalizing solutions that violate shallow mass divergence and moisture flux 
    convergence.
  - FR-2.5: The system shall generate multi-sample stochastic ensemble realizations 
    to provide $P_{50}$, $P_{90}$, and $P_{99}$ probabilistic threat layers.

5.3 Module 3: Visualization & Alerting Services
  - FR-3.1: The system shall render an interactive web GIS map displaying 
    synoptic anomaly tracks, bounding boxes, and downscaled 5 km layers.
  - FR-3.2: The system shall calculate the maximum amplitude centroid coordinate 
    and output a categorized circular alert with a 5 km impact radius.
  - FR-3.3: The system shall expose a REST API delivering GeoJSON payloads and 
    Cloud Optimized GeoTIFF (COG) rasters.


6. NON-FUNCTIONAL REQUIREMENTS (NFR)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6.1 Performance & Scalability
  - NFR-1.1: Stage 1 GNN trajectory inference across a 10-day forecast horizon 
    shall execute in <= 10 seconds.
  - NFR-1.2: Stage 2 diffusion sampling for a 500 km x 500 km region shall 
    complete within <= 45 seconds using accelerated DDIM sampling ($S \le 50$).
  - NFR-1.3: Total end-to-end execution time from crop to alert generation 
    shall not exceed 60 seconds on a single NVIDIA A10G or equivalent GPU.

6.2 System Reliability & Fault Tolerance
  - NFR-2.1: The ingestion engine shall handle missing or corrupted ensemble 
    members gracefully by re-normalizing weights across available members.
  - NFR-2.2: If the generative diffusion model fails a physics sanity check 
    (residual threshold exceeded), the system shall automatically fall back to 
    a deterministic high-resolution topographic interpolation flagged with 
    "degraded_mode: true".

6.3 Standards Compliance & Security
  - NFR-3.1: All external spatial outputs shall conform strictly to the GeoJSON 
    RFC 7946 standard and OGC raster conventions.
  - NFR-3.2: API endpoints shall support role-based authentication (OAuth2 / API 
    keys) and strict rate-limiting to prevent Denial of Service.
